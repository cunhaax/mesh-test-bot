Automatic range tests for your Meshtastic network

A small bot that sends a few messages on a channel at random moments, listens for everyone else's, and writes a report of who was heard, over how many hops, with what signal — so you don't have to babysit a laptop to test coverage.

What it does

📤 Sends, on a schedule

A few short messages on your test channel, spread at random moments so a whole community doesn't transmit at once — or on a fixed interval, your choice.

👂 Listens the whole time

One long-lived connection to the radio hears everyone else running the same format, recording hops, SNR, RSSI, and whether a copy came via MQTT.

📝 Writes a report

A readable text file and a JSON line per session — delivery rate, duplicates, relay hints, and any connection outages during the test.

🤖 Runs unattended

Docker image for amd64, arm64 and arm/v7 (Raspberry Pi included). Repeats itself every week, recovers from radio reboots and WiFi drops on its own.

What a report looks like

Session 2026-09-26 21:00 → 21:30 | Reporter: AB12 (!deadbeef) | Place: City | Mode: LONG_FAST | Channel: 1 (TestChannel) | Sent: 3/3
node | name | mode | received | duplicates | hops | avg SNR | avg RSSI | place | via | note
!cafef00d | CD34 | LONG_FAST | 2/3 | 1 | 0 | 5.3 | -96 | Villatown | RF | direct
!0a1b2c3d | EF56 | LONG_FAST | 3/3 | 0 | 1-2 | -2.5 | -112 | Valley | RF | relay 0xe9

One row per station heard, each carrying:

  • 📍 Where they are — the place name that station itself reports, not guessed from the radio.
  • 📊 Delivery rate — how many of its messages arrived, and duplicate readings of the same one.
  • 📶 Hop range, SNR and RSSI — best and worst seen during the session.
  • 🌐 RF or MQTT — a copy that only arrived via MQTT is flagged and never counted as RF reach.
  • 🔁 A relay hint — which node likely repeated it, when the packet made more than one hop.

Plus a matching report.jsonl line per session, meant for a server to pick up later — reports stay local for now, but a future version may let you opt in to submitting them somewhere, for network-wide coverage stats across everyone running the bot.

Get started

One command, if you already have Docker:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cunhaax/mesh-test-bot/master/install/install.sh | sh

It asks 4 questions — the radio's IP, the test channel's index and name, and your city — and starts the bot. See the README for installing from source or without Docker.

Already running MeshMonitor? Skip the radio's IP and point the bot at its Virtual Node instead — see the MeshMonitor guide.

📐 Size your test

LoRa has no real collision avoidance, so many stations sharing a short window can flood the channel — the very congestion a range test is meant to measure. Check how much of your window a coordinated test would actually use:

Rough model: the standard LoRa time-on-air formula (Semtech AN1200.13) times an estimated number of on-air retransmissions. Not a guarantee, and not enforced by the bot itself — message_count is capped at 5 and listen_minutes has a 120-minute floor regardless of this calculator (see the README's Random-schedule limits).
